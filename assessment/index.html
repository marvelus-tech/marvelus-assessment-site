What we talk about on the call

The voice agent follows your answers. If you mention chasing invoices, it asks how often, who does it and what gets delayed. These are the five areas it covers.

Sales and enquiries
How leads arrive, how fast you reply, where follow-up drops off and how many turn into work.
From sold to delivered
The steps between a yes and a finished job. Where rework happens and where customers wait.
Hidden labour
The routine tasks that eat your week: email back and forth, data entry, chasing documents, writing the same proposal again.
The money side
What you spend on software, what an hour of your time is worth, and what you would do with ten hours back.
Limits to growth
What breaks if work doubles, who you would hire next and what keeps you working weekends.

What the report measures

Five dimensions, one ladder.

A real assessment scores five dimensions, each on a five-point maturity ladder from ad hoc to optimised. The headline number matters less than which dimension is lowest. That is where the recommendations should point.

Tooling
Whether the software running your core work is capable, and what AI is already in use.
Data readiness
Whether your data is in one place, clean and reachable, or trapped in exports and inboxes.
Process maturity
Whether workflows are defined and repeatable, or live in individual people's heads.
Automation
How much already runs without someone moving it along by hand.
Team and AI literacy
Whether your people are ready to use it, and whether leadership is driving it.

The ladder

  1. 1. Ad hoc Done differently each time, if at all.
  2. 2. Emerging Some pieces exist, but they are patchy.
  3. 3. Defined Written down and mostly followed.
  4. 4. Managed Measured and checked on a regular basis.
  5. 5. Optimised Tuned over time, with little manual effort.

Common worries

"I do not have 15 minutes."

The call is about 15. If you are working weekends or chasing the same admin every week, you are already spending far more time than that. The call shows you where it goes.

"We already use project management software."

Software does not diagnose how work moves. You can have good tools and still lose a day a week to handovers and chasing. The call looks for the pattern, not a missing app.

"What about privacy?"

What you tell us stays with you and us. We do not sell it, share it or reuse it, and the transcript is yours. The call exists to map your operation, not to collect your data.

"What if it is not worth it?"

Weigh the fee against the size of the leak. A task that costs you 18 hours a week at $50 an hour is about $46,800 a year in time. If your report finds something that size, the fee is small next to it. If it finds nothing worth fixing, we say so. We make no promises about results.

What does the assessment measure?

It measures how your business runs today and where the effort goes, across tooling, data readiness, process maturity, automation, and team and AI literacy. The score gives you one number. The more useful part is which dimension is lowest, because that is where the first fixes belong.

Why does it not start with AI?

Because speeding up a broken process only breaks it faster. We work in the same order every time, and AI comes last.

  1. Question the requirement. Does this work need to happen at all?
  2. Delete. Remove what should not be there.
  3. Simplify and standardise. Make what is left the same way every time.
  4. Speed up the handovers. Cut the waiting between people and between systems.
  5. Automate last. Only where a person does not need to decide.

How do you work out the cost?

We keep it simple. Hours a week × hourly rate × 4.33 gives you a monthly figure. Multiply by 12 for the year.

Hours only turn into money in three ways: you stop paying for something, you avoid a hire you were about to make, or you serve demand that is already waiting. We count one of those per workflow, never two. Where a figure involves revenue, we use gross profit, not revenue. Every figure traces to something you told us. Where we do not know a number, the report marks it unknown instead of guessing. If the honest answer is that the saved hours just disappear into the day, the report says so and shows zero.

What happens after the report?

It is yours. Some people act on it alone. Others ask us to help, with digital support, SEO or other work from the report. Either is fine. See case studies and pricing.
